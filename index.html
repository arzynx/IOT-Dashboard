01 / Core concepts

01

What is IoT?

IoT means Internet of Things: everyday objects that can sense what is happening, send information online, and sometimes be controlled from far away. Think of it like giving ordinary things a way to notice, speak, and listen through the internet.

That can make little daily tasks easier, safer, or more efficient.

  • Smart home lights you control from your phone
  • A fitness band that tracks your steps
  • A smart water meter that reports usage
  • Automatic street lights that switch on at dusk
  • A car GPS tracker that shares its location
02

What are Embedded Systems?

An embedded system is a tiny computer built into a device to do one specific job. A microwave timer, a washing machine cycle, and an Arduino or ESP32 guiding a robot all use embedded systems.

Here is the key difference: embedded systems are the device’s body and brain; IoT is what happens when that brain connects to the internet. A device can be embedded without being online, but connecting it can turn it into an IoT device.

03

How It All Works

  1. Sensor collects data such as temperature, light, or movement.
  2. Microcontroller processes it and decides what the data means.
  3. Data travels over Wi-Fi or a network when it needs to go somewhere else.
  4. The cloud stores or analyses it so it can be useful over time.
  5. You see it in an app or an action happens, such as a light switching on.

02 / Hardware library

Meet the Sensors

Sensors are the parts that help a device notice the world around it.

Temperature (DHT11 / LM35)

Measures the heat in the surrounding air.

Used in: weather stations and smart ACs.

LDR (Light Dependent Resistor)

Measures how bright or dark a place is.

Used in: automatic street lights and phone auto-brightness.

Ultrasonic (HC-SR04)

Measures distance by sending out sound waves.

Used in: car parking sensors and obstacle-avoiding robots.

PIR Motion

Notices movement from the heat given off by bodies.

Used in: security alarms and automatic doors.

Humidity (DHT22)

Measures how much moisture is in the air.

Used in: greenhouses and smart farming.

Gas / Smoke (MQ-2)

Measures the amount of gas or smoke in the air.

Used in: fire alarms and kitchen leak detectors.
